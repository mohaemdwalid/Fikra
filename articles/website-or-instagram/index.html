Business websites

Is Instagram enough, or does your business need a website?

By FIKRA · Updated 16 October 2026 · 2 min read

You can keep Instagram as a discovery channel and use a website for details or purchases. They do not have to compete for the same job.

In this guide
  1. When messages become the bottleneck
  2. Identify the repeated work
  3. Protect the buying context
  4. Maintain both deliberately
  5. Give each channel a clear job

When messages become the bottleneck

Imagine promoting a product and answering its size, delivery and price questions separately for every interested person. A matching product page can put those answers and the purchase step together. If your service genuinely needs a conversation, keep that route, but give people useful context first. Link a specific post to a relevant destination instead of making visitors search through the profile or homepage to find the item again.

Identify the repeated work

Review messages about prices, product options, delivery or booking. If the same answers are scattered across posts, a permanent page can make the next step clearer. Keep social content linked to the relevant destination.

Protect the buying context

A product advert should lead to that product or a matching explanation. Sending every visitor to a generic profile can require them to search again. Test the route as someone who has never spoken to you.

Maintain both deliberately

Use social media for discovery and conversation, then keep approved details on pages you can update consistently. Account access, response ownership and working links matter on both surfaces. Neither follower count nor a new domain proves sales demand.

Give each channel a clear job

Use social posts for discovery and conversation where they work for your audience, then give interested people a stable place to read details or buy. Keep product information consistent. A website needs upkeep and visitors too, so define the role before treating it as a substitute for all social activity.
